# Scenario: Instructional Designer Interviews a Client About Course Levels 1–4

## Purpose
Use this role-play scenario in a training course to model how an instructional designer can guide a client to choose the right level of course build (Level 1, 2, 3, or 4).

## Characters
- **Jordan (Instructional Designer)**: Consultative, asks clarifying questions, explains options clearly.
- **Taylor (Client/Stakeholder)**: Needs a compliance and skills course for regional managers.

## Scene Setup
Taylor has requested "an eLearning course" but has not specified the level of complexity. Jordan schedules a discovery call to determine the best fit.

---

## Scripted Interaction

**Jordan (ID):** Thanks for meeting today, Taylor. Before we scope the project, I’d like to understand what outcomes you need and then align on the right build level—Level 1, 2, 3, or 4. Sound good?

**Taylor (Client):** Perfect. I’ve heard those terms, but I’m not clear on what each level includes.

**Jordan (ID):** Great place to start. Quick overview:
- **Level 1:** Basic presentation-style eLearning. Mostly click-next content, simple knowledge checks.
- **Level 2:** Adds moderate interactivity—click-to-reveal, labeled graphics, branched knowledge checks.
- **Level 3:** Scenario-based interactions with decision-making, realistic feedback, and stronger practice.
- **Level 4:** High-end simulation or game-based experience with complex branching, custom media, and advanced interactivity.

**Taylor (Client):** That helps. We need managers to apply policy in tricky employee situations, not just memorize it.

**Jordan (ID):** Good indicator. Let me ask a few questions.
1. Is the primary goal awareness, understanding, or behavior change on the job?
2. What happens if someone applies the policy incorrectly?
3. Do learners need to practice decisions in a safe environment?

**Taylor (Client):**
1. Definitely behavior change.
2. Incorrect decisions could create legal risk.
3. Yes, practice would be very valuable.

**Jordan (ID):** Understood. That usually points us beyond Level 1 and 2. What timeline and budget range are you working with?

**Taylor (Client):** We need launch in 10 weeks. Budget is moderate—not huge, but we can invest if impact is clear.

**Jordan (ID):** Helpful. Do you have existing assets—SMEs, policy documentation, brand templates, video, or case studies?

**Taylor (Client):** We have SMEs and policy docs. No polished case videos.

**Jordan (ID):** Based on your goals and constraints, here’s my recommendation:
- **Best fit: Level 3** scenario-based course.
- We can build realistic manager conversations with consequences and coaching feedback.
- This supports behavior change and risk reduction, while staying feasible for your 10-week timeline.

**Taylor (Client):** Why not Level 4?

**Jordan (ID):** Level 4 would be great for immersive simulation, but it typically needs more budget, more custom media production, and longer development cycles. For your timeline, Level 3 gives strong performance impact without overextending scope.

**Taylor (Client):** Makes sense. Could we include a few short videos later?

**Jordan (ID):** Absolutely. We can design Level 3 now and plan a phased enhancement path:
- Phase 1: Launch core Level 3 scenarios.
- Phase 2: Add optional video-based branches.
- Phase 3: Evaluate if a Level 4 module is justified for high-risk topics.

**Taylor (Client):** Let’s proceed with Level 3.

**Jordan (ID):** Great. I’ll document scope, interaction map, and success metrics so we can confirm alignment before development starts.

---

## Facilitator Notes (for use in your course)

### Key behaviors to model
- Clarifies definitions of Levels 1–4 in plain language.
- Uses consultative questions before recommending a solution.
- Connects recommendation to **business risk**, **timeline**, and **budget**.
- Offers a phased roadmap instead of a binary yes/no recommendation.

### Debrief prompts for learners
1. Which client answers most strongly indicated Level 3?
2. What would have to change for Level 2 to be enough?
3. What signals would justify Level 4 despite higher cost?
4. How did the designer balance ideal pedagogy with real constraints?

### Optional branching variation
If you want this interaction to become a branching scenario in your course, create decision points where the learner (as Jordan) chooses how to respond:
- Explain all levels first vs. ask discovery questions first.
- Recommend Level 2 vs. Level 3 vs. Level 4.
- Offer a phased plan vs. single-phase plan.

Then provide feedback showing consequences for credibility, stakeholder confidence, and project success.
